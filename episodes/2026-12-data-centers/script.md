## PART 1: WHAT'S BEING BUILT
**N04:** Start with the one that's already moving. Fluidstack, a New York company that builds computing capacity for AI firms, broke ground in September on a campus between Valley International Airport and the Port of Harlingen, near Rio Hondo.
(KRGV, Sep 2026)
**N05:** The company says the first phase will employ more than twenty-five hundred construction workers and several hundred permanent staff, and it's targeting completion in mid twenty twenty-eight.
(KRGV; Construction Review Online, Sep 2026)
**N06:** Fluidstack is also doing something unusual for a project this size: it isn't asking for local property tax breaks. It says it will pay for its own power line and substation from AEP Texas, and cool its servers with a closed loop of water from the East Rio Hondo Water Supply Corporation, filled once and recycled.
(KRGV, Sep 2026, Fluidstack statement)
**N07:** That didn't make it simple. Harlingen's commissioners tabled a deal to send the site's stormwater into a city canal after residents pushed back. The county issued the permit instead, and Harlingen's mayor says twenty million dollars in proposed infrastructure money will now go to the county rather than the city.
(KRGV, Sep 2026: Mayor Norma Sepulveda statement)
**N08:** The second project is still on paper. Eneus Energy, a British developer, has proposed a campus of up to two gigawatts on about seventeen hundred eighty-five acres near Harlingen. A company-commissioned study puts the potential investment at fourteen billion dollars, but its option on the land runs through July twenty twenty-seven, and nothing has been bought.
(Baxtel; BeBeez, Feb 2026; data center trackers, Apr-May 2026; label PROPOSED on screen)

## PART 2: THE SPACEX PLAN
**N09:** And then there's SpaceX. Inside the city of Starbase, the company wants to build a twenty-four-billion-dollar computing complex with its own natural gas power plant, about seven hundred forty-eight megawatts, so it doesn't lean on the state grid.
(RGV Business Journal, Nov 11, 2026; S&P Global, Nov 2026)
**N10:** It would sit on four hundred forty-four acres, the same land Brownsville gave up earlier this year in exchange for a two-hundred-twenty-million-dollar investment in the region's water supply.
(RGV Business Journal, Nov 11, 2026)
**N11:** SpaceX asked the county for a ten-year, one hundred percent property tax abatement on a project its application values at about thirteen point eight billion dollars for tax purposes.
(RGV Business Journal / MyRGV, Nov 17, 2026, citing the application)
**N12:** On November seventeenth, commissioners voted three to two not to even schedule a public hearing. County Judge Eddie Treviño and Commissioner Joey Lopez voted yes. Commissioners Sofia Benavidez, David Garza and Gus Ruiz voted no.
(MyRGV; ValleyCentral; RGV Business Journal, Nov 17, 2026)
**N13:** SpaceX answered the next day. It argued that computing equipment loses value so fast that it could make local tax rates swing from year to year, and it warned it could send future investment to sites outside Cameron County.
(RGV Business Journal, Nov 18, 2026; MyRGV, Nov 19, 2026)
**N14:** It isn't over. The city of Starbase, incorporated around the launch site last year, holds its own public hearing on a separate tax deal on December ninth. That's the next date to watch.
(MyRGV; RGV Business Journal, Nov 2026)

## PART 3: TEXAS HITS THE BRAKES
**N15:** All of this is happening as the state turns against data centers. In August, Governor Abbott ordered the grid operator, ERCOT, to audit every data center waiting to connect. In September, he told the state environmental agency to stop issuing data center permits until that audit is done.
(Office of the Governor, Sep 21, 2026; Gibson Dunn, Nov 2026)
**N16:** His conditions are blunt: data centers must pay for their own electrical infrastructure, can't use water local communities need, and must report how much power and water they use. ERCOT expects to finish its audit in mid-December.
(Office of the Governor, Sep 21, 2026; Gibson Dunn, Nov 2026)
**N17:** Then the election changed the picture again. Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa, who takes office January nineteenth, has promised a moratorium on new data center approvals until the Legislature passes rules on the grid, water and local impacts, and she wants to end the state's tax breaks for them.
(Texas Tribune, Nov 4, 2026; Houston Chronicle, Nov 2026)
**N18:** The Legislature convenes January twelfth. Analysts at Jefferies estimate about thirteen gigawatts of announced Texas projects are now at risk.
(Texas Tribune, Nov 2026; Gas Turbine World citing Jefferies)
**N19:** The key question is what happens to projects already in the ground. Hinojosa has said that anything still needing a state permit would be on hold. Energy analysts expect most projects already under construction, with their permits and grid connections in hand, to keep going.
(Houston Chronicle Q&A, Nov 2026; Texas Tribune, Nov 2026)

## PART 4: WHAT IT MEANS FOR THE VALLEY
**N20:** For the Valley, the stakes go well beyond the data centers themselves. Twenty-five hundred construction workers near Rio Hondo need places to live, eat and park, the same kind of demand the LNG build created around Port Isabel.
**N21:** The tax base matters too. A data center that pays full property taxes adds millions a year for the county, school districts and other local taxing units. One that wins a full abatement adds very little for a decade.
**N22:** And then there's water. This is a region that just spent two years worrying about the Rio Grande. Every one of these projects says it will use closed-loop cooling or reclaimed water, and the state is now demanding they prove it.
(RGV News Episode 2; Office of the Governor, Sep 2026)

## PART 5: WHAT TO WATCH
**N23:** If you're buying land or building near Rio Hondo and the airport corridor, Fluidstack is the one to plan around. It's permitted, under construction and paying its own way, so watch its hiring and its community benefits agreement with the county.
**N24:** If you're betting on Starbase, wait for December ninth, and for the new governor's first order in January. A project that still needs state permits could sit for months.
**N25:** And if you're lending, treat the proposed projects as proposals. Eneus hasn't bought its land, and SpaceX has already said it could build elsewhere.
**N26:** Our watch list: the Starbase hearing on December ninth, ERCOT's audit in mid-December, and the Legislature opening January twelfth.
