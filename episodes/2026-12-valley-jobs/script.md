**N00:** Cameron County says just four projects, two LNG export terminals, SpaceX's Starbase and a new shipyard, add up to more than forty-one billion dollars in investment. Some regional tallies go past a hundred billion.
*KINETIC: $41.1 BILLION · 4 PROJECTS*
(RGV Business Journal, Oct 3, 2026, citing Cameron County Economic Development; Strategic Partnerships Inc., Nov 2026)
**N01:** And yet, in the three months through September, the Rio Grande Valley lost jobs. The Dallas Fed says employment here fell at an annualized rate of about six tenths of a percent, roughly seven hundred jobs, while Texas and the country still grew.
*KINETIC: -0.6% · ABOUT 700 JOBS*
(Federal Reserve Bank of Dallas, Rio Grande Valley Economic Indicators, Q3 2026)
**N02:** That's the puzzle for anyone building here right now. The money is real, but the paychecks haven't caught up, and where they show up first decides where demand for homes, rentals and stores shows up next.
**N03:** So is the boom real, where is it actually landing, and what should builders, buyers and lenders watch before twenty twenty-seven? This is RGV News.

## PART 1: THE MONEY ON PAPER
[VISUAL: port flyover over the LNG site; bar chart of the county's four projects; Starbase orbit]
**N04:** Start with the money. Cameron County's economic development office lists its four biggest projects as Rio Grande LNG, Texas LNG, SpaceX's Starbase operations and Saronic's Port Alpha shipyard. By the county's own figures, Rio Grande LNG alone is about thirty-two billion dollars.
*ON SCREEN: Rio Grande LNG ~$31.8B · total of four ~$41.1B (Cameron County estimates)*
(RGV Business Journal, Oct 3, 2026)
**N05:** But those projects are at very different stages. Rio Grande LNG is under construction: its developer, NextDecade, says the first two trains were about eighty percent complete in September, and it expects its first LNG in the second quarter of twenty twenty-seven.
(NextDecade third quarter 2026 business update, Oct 29, 2026)
**N06:** Texas LNG has pushed back its final investment decision for two years running. And Saronic's shipyard won a ninety-five percent county tax abatement in June, with the phases tied to that deal starting in twenty twenty-nine.
(RGV Business Journal, Oct 3, 2026; KSAT and Cameron County, Jun 2026; USNI News, Jul 16, 2026)
**N07:** So when you hear a number like a hundred ten billion dollars for the region, remember what it is: a tally of projects that are underway, approved or simply announced. It's not money that has already been spent, and it's not paychecks that have already been written.
(Strategic Partnerships Inc., Nov 2026; San Antonio Express-News)

## PART 2: THE JOBS ON THE GROUND
[VISUAL: USGS aerials of McAllen and Brownsville; line chart of quarterly job growth Q1-Q3 2026]
**N08:** Now look at the jobs. Earlier this year, the Valley was still adding them: about one point four percent annualized in the first quarter and one point three in the second. In the third quarter, that flipped to a decline.
*ON SCREEN: Q1 +1.4% · Q2 +1.3% · Q3 -0.6% (annualized)*
(Federal Reserve Bank of Dallas, RGV Economic Indicators, Q1-Q3 2026)
**N09:** And the two halves of the Valley are moving in opposite directions. Over the year through October, the McAllen metro added about forty-five hundred jobs, mostly in health care and education. The Brownsville metro, the home of the LNG and SpaceX boom, lost about three hundred.
*ON SCREEN: McAllen +4,500 (to 311,800) · Brownsville -300*
(RGV Business Journal, Nov 20, 2026, citing Texas Workforce Commission data)
**N10:** In the third quarter alone, the Dallas Fed says Brownsville's employment shrank at an annualized rate of almost three percent, while McAllen still grew a little.
*ON SCREEN: Brownsville -2.8% · McAllen +0.6%*
(Federal Reserve Bank of Dallas, Q3 2026)
**N11:** Here's the part that's easy to misread. Unemployment actually fell, to six percent in Brownsville and five point eight in McAllen, against four point four statewide. But the Dallas Fed notes that part of that drop comes from a shrinking labor force. In Brownsville, the labor force is down about two percent from a year ago.
(Federal Reserve Bank of Dallas, Q3 2026)
**N12:** A falling jobless rate usually sounds like good news. Here, some of it simply means fewer people are working or looking for work.

## PART 3: WHY THE PAYCHECKS LAG
[VISUAL: Pharr bridge orbit; wage comparison bars]
**N13:** So why isn't all that investment showing up in payrolls? This month in McAllen, Dallas Fed senior business economist Luis Torres told Valley business leaders that the weak hiring says more about the supply of workers than about a weak economy.
(RGV Business Journal, Nov 18, 2026)
**N14:** He said labor force growth has been flat or even negative, that immigration policies have kept some workers without U.S. citizenship out of certain industries, and that wage growth is still solid.
(RGV Business Journal, Nov 18, 2026)
**N15:** The numbers back him up. The Valley's economy grew about two point six percent in twenty twenty-five, but jobs grew just two tenths of a percent. The Dallas Fed calls it a low-hire, low-fire economy, where output rises without much new hiring.
*ON SCREEN: 2025 output +2.6% · jobs +0.2%*
(RGV Business Journal, Feb 15, 2026, citing the Dallas Fed and economist Pia Orrenius)
**N16:** And pay is still the lowest in Texas. In twenty twenty-five, the median annual wage in the workforce area covering Hidalgo and Starr counties was about thirty-six thousand dollars, the lowest in the state. Cameron County was second-lowest, at about thirty-seven thousand. Statewide, it was about forty-eight thousand six hundred.
*ON SCREEN: Hidalgo/Starr $35,971 · Cameron $36,942 · Texas $48,621*
(RGV Business Journal, Aug 2, 2026, citing a Texas Workforce Commission report)
**N17:** The big projects also hire on their own clock. As we covered in Episode 3, Rio Grande LNG was approved for a peak of about seventy-five hundred construction workers, but expects about seven hundred permanent jobs once it runs. Saronic says its shipyard could create up to ten thousand jobs, over a decade.
(FERC, Apr 2026, and NextDecade via Episode 3 sources; Defense News and USNI News, Jul 2026)

## PART 4: THE CRACKS TO WATCH
[VISUAL: Donna bridge at golden hour (trucks and trade); TAHC zone map]
**N18:** There are also cracks worth watching. Two Valley trucking companies, Xoco Transport of Hidalgo and American Petroleum Carriers of McAllen, filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in recent weeks, and three more carriers filed in Laredo.
(RGV Business Journal, Nov 23, 2026)
**N19:** Court records show steep drops in revenue and heavy debt on their trucks, and the report points to immigration changes, record diesel prices and a sluggish economy. Across the Valley, business bankruptcies doubled last year, from sixty-two to a hundred twenty-seven.
(RGV Business Journal, Nov 23, 2026 and Mar 7, 2026)
**N20:** And this week, a new risk reached the ranches. Texas animal health officials confirmed New World screwworm in cattle in Hidalgo County on November twenty-first, then in Cameron County, and put parts of Cameron, Hidalgo and Willacy counties under animal movement restrictions.
(Texas Animal Health Commission order, Nov 23, 2026; RGV Business Journal, Nov 24 and Nov 26, 2026)
**N21:** For ranch land and the lenders behind it, that's a new line on the risk list, at least until the quarantine lifts.

## PART 5: WHAT IT MEANS FOR BUILDERS, BUYERS AND LENDERS
[VISUAL: SH 4 at sunset; Starbase orbit; checklist card]
**N22:** Not everything is slowing. Valley cities collected about six percent more sales tax over the year through October, so shoppers are still spending, though those numbers lag actual sales by about two months.
(RGV Business Journal, Nov 25, 2026, citing the Texas Comptroller)
**N23:** If you build, price for when workers arrive, not when projects are announced. In McAllen, demand is following health care and education jobs now. In Brownsville, it's tied to project timelines: first LNG in the second quarter of twenty twenty-seven, and Saronic's phases from twenty twenty-nine.
**N24:** If you lend, look hard at freight and trucking borrowers, at ranch exposure inside the screwworm zone, and at rental demand built around construction crews that will shrink once the LNG plant is running.
**N25:** And watch the calendar. The Dallas Fed updates its Texas jobs forecast every month, first gas is expected at Rio Grande LNG before the end of this year, and Pharr is moving to add three hundred eighty-four acres near its international bridge to its tax reinvestment zone by the end of December.
(Federal Reserve Bank of Dallas; NextDecade, Oct 29, 2026; RGV Business Journal, Nov 23, 2026)
**N26:** The boom is real on paper. The question for the next twelve months is how much of it turns into paychecks here. This is RGV News. This video is for information only and isn't financial advice.
