### PART 1 — WHAT'S BEING BUILT (0:50–3:00)

[VISUAL: animated map — Texas → Cameron County → Boca Chica]

**NARRATION:** Starbase sits at the very southern tip of Texas, on Boca Chica, about twenty miles east of Brownsville. A decade ago, this was a handful of beach houses at the end of a two-lane road.

*ON SCREEN (kinetic): 300 → 3,400+*

**NARRATION:** Today it's a company town. SpaceX's workforce here grew from about three hundred people in twenty twenty to more than thirty-four hundred by twenty twenty-four. And it isn't slowing down.

[VISUAL: Blender — Starbase flyover; production site + launch site labels]

**NARRATION:** Two projects are driving the next wave. The first is called GigaBay: a seven-hundred-thousand-square-foot factory, a five-hundred-and-six-million-dollar investment, built to produce Starship rockets at a pace SpaceX describes as a thousand per year.

*ON SCREEN: GIGABAY · $506M · 700,000 sq ft*

**NARRATION:** The second is a four-hundred-and-eighty-million-dollar expansion of launch infrastructure, including two launch pads, so SpaceX can fly more often.

*ON SCREEN: LAUNCH EXPANSION · $480M · 2 pads*

**NARRATION:** Each project is expected to bring about five hundred jobs, and SpaceX says at least a quarter of those positions will go to local hires.

*ON SCREEN (kinetic): 1,000 · NEW · JOBS*

**NARRATION:** For a region that's long been one of the poorest in Texas, that's a big deal. For anyone who builds or invests here, the question is simple: where are those thousand-plus workers going to live?

(Texas Tribune, Jan 29, 2026: workforce 300→3,400+; GigaBay 700,000 sq ft / 1,000 rockets per year; two pads. KSAT/Click2Houston via Texas Tribune and local reporting: $506M and $480M, 500 jobs each, 25% local hiring.)

### PART 2 — THE ROAD AND THE WATER (3:00–6:00)

[VISUAL: Blender — SH 4 glowing route Brownsville → Starbase; "ONE ROAD IN."]

**NARRATION:** Everything at Starbase depends on one road: State Highway 4, the two-lane highway from Brownsville to Boca Chica.

*ON SCREEN (kinetic): ONE · ROAD · IN.*

**NARRATION:** Texas plans to spend more than a hundred and ninety-two million dollars to widen about fourteen miles of it into a four-lane divided highway, rebuilt in concrete to handle heavy trucks. TxDOT says crash rates on this stretch run one and a half to two times the state average.

*ON SCREEN: SH 4 WIDENING · $192M+ · 14 miles · 2 → 4 lanes*

**NARRATION:** But here's the timeline that matters for developers: construction bids aren't expected until the fall of twenty twenty-seven, and the work could take two and a half to three years. So the road that everything depends on won't be finished until around twenty thirty.

*ON SCREEN: BIDS: FALL 2027 → DONE: ~2030*

[VISUAL: owner footage — beach traffic / crowds if available; otherwise SH 4 aerial]

**NARRATION:** Then there's water. In August, Brownsville's city commission held a six-hour Saturday meeting and voted unanimously to remove about four hundred and forty-five acres near the Port of Brownsville from the city limits, at SpaceX's request.

[VISUAL: map — Port of Brownsville area highlighted (general area, labeled approximate)]

**NARRATION:** In exchange, SpaceX agreed to pay the city up to two hundred and twenty million dollars for water projects. Forty million up front, then six quarterly payments of thirty million.

*ON SCREEN: UP TO $220M · $40M + 6 × $30M*

[VISUAL: animated chart — Brownsville water: 21 MGD today, 70% from Rio Grande; +8 MGD reuse; +10 MGD plant expansion]

**NARRATION:** That money pays for two projects: a wastewater reclamation plant adding about eight million gallons a day, and a water plant expansion adding about ten million more. Brownsville uses about twenty-one million gallons a day today, and seventy percent of that comes from the Rio Grande.

**NARRATION:** The mayor says the deal could reduce, or even eliminate, the city's reliance on the river. In a region where water is the quiet limit on growth, that's a major opportunity.

*ON SCREEN (kinetic): WHERE · WILL · THE · WATER · BE?*

**NARRATION:** But there was a catch. SpaceX said the offer was only good if the commission voted that same day. Residents asked why the city was rushing, and what SpaceX planned to do with the land. Even the mayor admitted, quote, "It is rushed. There's no doubt."

(Texas Tribune via KRGV, Aug 31, 2026, Brownsville de-annexation and water deal; KRGV, Aug 28, 2026, SH 4 widening: $192M+, 14 miles, crash rates, bids fall 2027, 2.5–3 years.)

### PART 3 — THE BIGGER PICTURE, AND THE RISKS (6:00–8:30)

[VISUAL: Blender — "SpaceX in Texas" pillars; "WHERE THE MONEY GOES."]

**NARRATION:** Now zoom out. SpaceX's biggest Texas announcement this year wasn't in the Valley at all. In Grimes County, northwest of Houston, the governor's office announced a chip plant SpaceX calls Terafab: more than sixteen point eight billion dollars in its first phase, with three thousand jobs.

*ON SCREEN: GRIMES COUNTY · $16.8B (phase 1) vs STARBASE · ~$1B*

**NARRATION:** And in August, SpaceX announced a hundred-billion-dollar launch site in Louisiana. Starbase is still the heart of Starship. But SpaceX now has options, and options mean leverage.

*ON SCREEN: LOUISIANA · $100B launch site (announced)*

**NARRATION:** You heard that leverage in Brownsville: take the deal today, or we'll look at our other facilities.

[VISUAL: text card — Starbase City Commission: 3 members, 2 SpaceX employees]

**NARRATION:** There are other questions too. Starbase is a city whose three-member commission includes two SpaceX employees, and it nominated SpaceX for up to seven and a half million dollars in state tax refunds, through a program meant for economically distressed areas. Critics, like the watchdog group Good Jobs First, ask whether a company worth hundreds of billions of dollars needs that help.

[VISUAL: owner footage — booster fireball, brief]

**NARRATION:** And then there's the nature of the business itself. This is a test program. In two days, we saw a booster lost and a ship caught. Setbacks can bring investigations and pauses, and launch schedules can shift. If you're betting on Starbase's growth, you're betting on a company still learning to fly.

(Office of the Texas Governor, Terafab announcement; Fortune, Aug 27, 2026, Louisiana; Texas Tribune, Jan 29, 2026, Starbase commission and tax refund, Good Jobs First quote.)

### PART 4 — WHAT IT MEANS FOR BUILDERS AND INVESTORS (8:30–9:45)

[VISUAL: map — Brownsville, Port Isabel, Laguna Vista, SH 4 corridor; icons for housing, industrial, water]

**NARRATION:** So what does this mean if you build, buy, or lend in the Valley?

*ON SCREEN: OPPORTUNITIES*

**NARRATION:** On the opportunity side: a thousand-plus new jobs need places to live, and Brownsville and the communities closest to Starbase are first in line for workforce housing demand. Suppliers, contractors, and industrial users follow big factories. And new water capacity, if it gets built, is what lets new subdivisions get permitted.

*ON SCREEN: RISKS TO WATCH*

**NARRATION:** On the risk side: much of this depends on one company. The road won't be wider until around twenty thirty. The water projects depend on SpaceX's payments arriving on schedule. And SpaceX is spreading its bets across Texas and beyond.

*ON SCREEN (kinetic): ASK · BEFORE · YOU · BUILD.*

**NARRATION:** Before you underwrite a project here, ask three questions. How much of your demand depends on Starbase? What's the water and road timeline for your site? And what happens to your numbers if launch schedules slip a year?
