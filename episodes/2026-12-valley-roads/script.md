## COLD OPEN
[VISUAL: USGS aerial pan along State Highway 4 through the wetlands to Starbase; then the Episode 1 Starbase flyover]
**N00:** In twenty nineteen, about six hundred forty-five vehicles a day used State Highway 4, the only road from Brownsville to Starbase. By twenty twenty-four, it was carrying more than sixty-seven hundred, ten times as many, on the same two lanes.
*KINETIC: 645 → 6,743 A DAY*
(RGV Business Journal, Dec 16, 2025 and Aug 26, 2026, citing TxDOT data)
**N01:** This August, Texas finally put a price on fixing it: one hundred ninety-two million dollars to turn it into a four-lane highway. The project is scheduled to go out for bids before next August, and construction would take years after that.
(RGV Business Journal, Aug 26, 2026; TxDOT 2027 Unified Transportation Program)
**N02:** That's the pattern across the Valley right now. Jobs and investment are arriving faster than the roads, bridges and highways built to carry them, and the gap decides where the next wave of growth can actually go.
**N03:** So which projects are funded, which are stuck, and what does that mean if you build, buy or lend in the Valley? This is RGV News.

## PART 1: THE ONE ROAD TO STARBASE
[VISUAL: USGS aerial of SH 4; traffic columns 2019 / 2023 / 2024 / 2050 projected, one slow camera move]
**N04:** Start with the road everyone at Starbase knows. State Highway 4 runs about fourteen miles through wildlife refuge and tidal flats. It floods, it has little shoulder, and it's full of potholes.
(RGV Business Journal, Dec 16, 2025)
**N05:** TxDOT's plan would widen it from two lanes to a divided four-lane highway between Brownsville and Starbase, inside the right-of-way the state already owns, so no new land has to be bought.
(RGV Business Journal, Dec 16, 2025 and Aug 26, 2026)
**N06:** And the traffic isn't slowing down. TxDOT projects about nineteen thousand seven hundred vehicles a day by twenty fifty, nearly three times today's count.
*ON SCREEN: 645 (2019) · 4,683 (2023) · 6,743 (2024) · ~19,700 (2050 projection)*
(TxDOT data via RGV Business Journal)
**N07:** Starbase has already approved commercial projects along the corridor, including Rio West, a mixed-use development with housing, retail and restaurants along the river. Cameron County's mobility chief wants the four lanes extended even farther west, to Farm-to-Market 511, where SpaceX commuters turn off toward the port.
(RGV Business Journal, Dec 16, 2025; Pete Sepulveda Jr., Cameron County)

## PART 2: ONE BRIDGE TO THE ISLAND
[VISUAL: USGS aerial of the Queen Isabella Causeway and Laguna Madre; map with the two proposed second-causeway routes]
**N08:** Now look north. South Padre Island has exactly one road in and out: the Queen Isabella Causeway. That's a problem every summer weekend, and a much bigger one during a hurricane evacuation.
(TxDOT, South Padre Island Second Access Project)
**N09:** A second causeway has been talked about for two decades. In late twenty twenty-three, the state took it over as State Highway 104, which opened the door to state funding, and TxDOT is now running the environmental study.
(KRGV; TxDOT; Texas Transportation Commission minute order, Dec 12, 2023)
**N10:** TxDOT has narrowed it to two routes, both about eight miles across the Laguna Madre, both four lanes. It estimates the bridge could cost up to a billion dollars and take five to six years to build.
(KRGV, TxDOT public meeting)
**N11:** County leaders say it would open the island's north end to development. But there's no construction start date yet, so anyone pricing land up there is betting on a timeline that runs well into the twenty-thirties.
(Cameron County Judge Eddie Treviño Jr. via KRGV; TxDOT)

## PART 3: THE TRUCKS AND THE BRIDGES
[VISUAL: USGS aerials of the Pharr-Reynosa and Donna-Rio Bravo bridges; map of the planned trade corridor]
**N12:** In Hidalgo County, the pressure comes from trucks. Thousands of tractor-trailers cross northbound at the Pharr International Bridge every day.
(RGV Business Journal, Feb 26, 2026)
**N13:** At the Donna bridge, a sixty-two-million-dollar project would put U.S. and Mexican inspectors in a single facility with six dedicated lanes. Officials call it the port of the future, and once it's finished, before the end of twenty twenty-eight, it could handle up to a hundred trucks an hour.
(RGV Business Journal, Feb 26, 2026)
**N14:** The catch is the road behind it. The International Bridge Trade Corridor, a toll-free, thirteen-mile truck route from the Pharr and Donna bridges to U.S. 281, was supposed to start construction this year. Governor Abbott asked TxDOT for a hundred fifty million dollars for it in twenty twenty-three. Local officials say the funding has hit a snag.
(RGV Business Journal, Feb 26, 2026; Donna interim city manager Jesus Reyna)
**N15:** Without it, a hundred trucks an hour would pour onto city streets in Donna, Alamo, San Juan and Pharr that were never built for that kind of weight.
**N16:** And it's not just Hidalgo County. The Port of Harlingen's director says the Valley has three heavyweight truck corridors, serving the Port of Harlingen, the Port of Brownsville and Hidalgo County, and none of them connect to each other.
(Walker Smith, Port of Harlingen, via RGV Business Journal, Feb 26, 2026)

## PART 4: THE INTERSTATE GAP
[VISUAL: map of I-69C, I-69E and I-2 in the Valley, with the unfinished link north highlighted]
**N17:** Then there's the biggest gap of all. The Valley has interstates, I-2, I-69 Central and I-69 East, but they still don't connect to the rest of the national interstate system. Interstate 69 East signs still end at Raymondville. North of there, TxDOT has about four hundred million dollars in contracts on U.S. 77 through Kenedy County, but that covers less than half of the miles still needed.
(TxDOT update via The Monitor, Jun 11, 2025: about $400M under contract in Kenedy County; Cameron County RMA I-69 update: 19.8 of the remaining 44 miles across Kenedy County)
**N18:** Closer to home, TxDOT is studying the I-69 Connector, a new east-west freeway between Edinburg and Harlingen. Its own fact sheet puts the final environmental decision in twenty twenty-seven, and Cameron County's mobility chief has said that if TxDOT won't build it, the counties should.
(TxDOT I-69 Connector fact sheet; Rio Grande Guardian, Feb 2026)

## PART 5: WHAT IT MEANS FOR BUILDERS, BUYERS AND LENDERS
[VISUAL: list cards over real aerials; watch-list card]
**N19:** So how do you use this? Start with what's funded. The State Highway 4 widening has a price tag and a bid year. The Donna bridge lanes have a completion date. Those are projects you can plan around.
**N20:** Then treat the rest as options, not promises. The second causeway, the trade corridor and the I-69 Connector all matter, but none has a construction start date, and land priced as if they're coming soon carries real timing risk.
**N21:** For builders, the near-term demand follows the trucks and the commuters: industrial and logistics space near the Pharr and Donna bridges, and housing and services along the Brownsville-to-Starbase corridor.
**N22:** For lenders, ask one question about any deal that depends on a new road: is it in the state's ten-year plan with real money behind it, or is it still in a study?
**N23:** Our watch list: the State Highway 4 bids, scheduled before next August, the Donna bridge construction schedule, the second causeway's environmental decision, and the Legislature, which opens January twelfth and controls the transportation money.
*ON SCREEN: SH 4 BIDS: BY AUG 2027 · DONNA BRIDGE: BY END OF 2028 · CAUSEWAY: ENVIRONMENTAL STUDY · LEGISLATURE: JAN 12*
**N24:** In the Valley, growth goes where the roads go. Right now, some of those roads are funded, and some are still lines on a map. This is RGV News.
*END CARD*
