## PART 1: THE GLUT (0:45-1:50)
[VISUAL: line chart, McAllen active listings 2019-2026, drawing in; then map of Hidalgo County subdivisions]
**N04:** Start with supply. Housing economists use months of inventory: how long it would take to sell every listed home at today's pace.
**N05:** Six months is balanced. Below six favors sellers. Above six favors buyers.
*ON SCREEN: months of inventory gauge, 2 → 6 → 9*
**N06:** In May twenty twenty-one, the McAllen metro had about two months of inventory. Homes got multiple offers. Buyers waived inspections.
(RGV Business Journal, Jul 7, 2026: about 757 active listings, roughly 2.2 months, May 2021)
**N07:** Builders answered that shortage. Hundreds of new subdivisions went up across Hidalgo County.
**N08:** Now the McAllen metro sits at about nine months of inventory. That's firmly a buyer's market.
(RGV Business Journal, Sep 25, 2026: "nearly 9 months of available inventory," August 2026)
**N09:** One private lender who finances builders put it bluntly. Some new subdivisions in Weslaco, he said, are "ghost towns, completely empty."
(Emiliano Zapata, Capital Insiders, via RGV Business Journal, Jan 8, 2026)

## PART 2: PRICES (1:50-2:50)
[VISUAL: 3D bar focus tour: McAllen median listing price, Oct 2021 / Oct 2023 peak / Oct 2026; then Brownsville]
**N10:** You might expect prices to crash. They haven't. They're slipping.
**N11:** The median listing price in the McAllen metro peaked at two hundred eighty thousand dollars in October twenty twenty-three. This October, it was two hundred fifty-five thousand. That's about nine percent lower.
(Realtor.com via FRED: MEDLISPRI32580)
**N12:** The median sale price has barely moved. It's held near two hundred forty-five thousand since twenty twenty-three.
(RGV Business Journal, Sep 25, 2026)
**N12b:** Homes are also sitting longer. In October, the typical McAllen listing stayed on the market about seventy-eight days. In the fall of twenty twenty-one, it was forty-eight.
(Realtor.com via FRED: median days on market, McAllen metro, Oct 2021: 48; Oct 2026: 78)
**N13:** What's changing is the discounting. In October, about one in six McAllen listings had a price cut.
(Realtor.com via FRED: 540 price reductions out of 3,432 active listings, Oct 2026)
**N14:** Builders are adding rate buy-downs, appliance packages, and warranties. In some cases, new homes are priced below resale homes.
(Brian Reed, Ryan & Brian Real Estate Team, via RGV Business Journal, Feb 25, 2026)
**N14b:** One example: a new three-bedroom on Gumwood Avenue in McAllen listed at two hundred seventy-five thousand in February. By August, the asking price was down to two fifty-nine.
(RGV Business Journal, Sep 25, 2026)

## PART 3: THE RATE WHIPLASH (2:50-3:55)
[VISUAL: line chart, Freddie Mac 30-year rate 2025-2026, the climb from 5.98% to 7.40%]
**N15:** Earlier this year, buyers caught a break. In late February, the thirty-year fixed rate dipped to five point nine eight percent.
(Freddie Mac PMMS via FRED: 5.98% on Feb 26, 2026)
**N16:** McAllen sales jumped. In January, closings were up twenty-one percent from a year earlier.
(RGV Business Journal, Feb 25, 2026, citing Texas A&M Real Estate Center data)
**N17:** Then inflation came back. Energy prices climbed with the conflict involving Iran, and rates followed. By October, they were back above seven percent.
(NAHB Eye on Housing, Nov 2026; Freddie Mac)
**N18:** Here's what that means in dollars. Take a two-hundred-fifty-five-thousand-dollar home with ten percent down. At the February rate, principal and interest is about thirteen hundred seventy a month. At today's rate, it's about fifteen hundred seventy.
*ON SCREEN: $1,373 → $1,570 / month · +$197 · about $2,400 a year (RGV News calculation: $229,500 loan, 30-year fixed, principal and interest only)*
**N19:** That's about two hundred dollars more every month, before taxes and insurance.
**N20:** And sales cooled. In September, McAllen metro sales were down eight percent from a year earlier.
(RGV Business Journal, Oct 22, 2026: 333 vs 361 sales)

## PART 4: THE AFFORDABILITY GAP (3:55-4:55)
[VISUAL: two diverging lines, home prices vs household income, 2016-2026; document highlight of the RGVBJ figures]
**N21:** The deeper problem is income. Over the past decade, median home prices rose seventy-five percent in the McAllen metro. Median household income rose sixty-three percent.
(RGV Business Journal, Jul 7, 2026)
**N22:** In Brownsville-Harlingen, prices more than doubled. Incomes rose sixty-seven percent.
**N23:** The median household in Hidalgo County earns about fifty-six thousand dollars a year. Spend thirty percent on housing, and that's about fourteen hundred a month. At today's rates, principal and interest alone on a median-priced home is more than that.
(U.S. Census Bureau, Small Area Income and Poverty Estimates, 2024: $55,829, via FRED; RGV News calculation)
**N24:** Then add property taxes and insurance. One Brownsville broker says deals fall apart when buyers see a monthly note near twenty-eight hundred dollars.
(Craig Grove, GRT Realty, via RGV Business Journal, Feb 25, 2026)
**N24b:** Taxes are also pushing more homes onto the market. One Valley realtor says owners now tell her they can't keep paying the property taxes on their salary, so they're selling.
(Myssie Cardenas-Barajas via RGV Business Journal, Jul 7, 2026)
**N25:** Realtors say the real demand is between one hundred fifty and two hundred fifty thousand dollars. But with lots now costing fifty to seventy thousand, builders say a two-hundred-thousand-dollar house is nearly impossible.
(Myssie Cardenas-Barajas via RGVBJ, Jul 7, 2026; Emiliano Zapata via RGVBJ, Jan 8, 2026)

## PART 5: TWO DIFFERENT MARKETS (4:55-5:35)
[VISUAL: map, Hidalgo vs Cameron counties; side-by-side gauges 9 months vs 6.7 months]
**N26:** And the Valley isn't one market. Brownsville-Harlingen has far fewer listings, about fifteen hundred, and roughly six point seven months of inventory. That's close to balanced.
(RGV Business Journal, Sep 25, 2026, August 2026 data)
**N26b:** Prices there are higher, though. The median Brownsville-Harlingen listing was two hundred ninety-seven thousand dollars in October, down from a peak of three twenty-one in twenty twenty-two.
(Realtor.com via FRED: MEDLISPRI15180, Oct 2022: $321,000; Oct 2026: $297,000)
**N27:** But Brownsville sales fell seventeen percent through August, back to twenty-nineteen levels, even with SpaceX and LNG hiring nearby.
(RGV Business Journal, Sep 25, 2026)

## PART 6: WHAT IT MEANS FOR BUILDERS, BUYERS AND LENDERS (5:35-6:40)
**N28:** So what should you do before twenty twenty-seven?
**N29:** If you're buying, this is leverage. Nine months of inventory means you can ask for price cuts, closing costs, and rate buy-downs.
**N30:** If you're building, the gap is the product. Townhomes, smaller homes, and build-to-rent can reach the one-fifty to two-fifty range that resale can't.
**N31:** If you're lending, watch carry costs. Banks are tightening construction loans. Some builders now pay private lenders around thirteen percent, versus about eight at a bank. A home that sits for nine months gets expensive.
(Emiliano Zapata via RGV Business Journal, Jan 8, 2026)
**N32:** Our watch list: weekly mortgage rates, monthly inventory in McAllen, and whether Brownsville sales recover as port and SpaceX payrolls grow.
**N33:** The Valley isn't crashing. It's resetting. The deals go to whoever does the math first. This is RGV News.
*END CARD*
