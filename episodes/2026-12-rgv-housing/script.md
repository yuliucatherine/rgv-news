## ON THE RECORD (montage, about 6 s; same four RGVBJ headlines as v1)

## PART 1: THE GLUT
[VISUAL: line chart of McAllen active listings drawing in; real photos of new homes and streets; months-of-inventory columns rising in order with one slow camera move]
**N04:** Start with supply. Housing economists measure it in months of inventory: how long it would take to sell every home on the market at the current pace of sales. Six months is considered balanced. Less than that favors sellers, and more than that favors buyers.
*KINETIC: 6 MONTHS = BALANCED*
**N05:** In May twenty twenty-one, at the height of the pandemic boom, the McAllen metro had roughly two months of inventory. Homes drew multiple offers, and buyers were waiving inspections.
(RGV Business Journal, Jul 7, 2026: about 757 active listings, roughly 2.2 months, May 2021)
**N06:** Builders responded the way you'd expect, and hundreds of new subdivisions went up across Hidalgo County.
**N07:** Now the pendulum has swung the other way. By August, the McAllen metro had nearly nine months of inventory, firmly in buyer's-market territory.
(RGV Business Journal, Sep 25, 2026)
**N08:** One private lender who finances local builders put it bluntly earlier this year. Some new subdivisions in Weslaco, he said, are "ghost towns, completely empty." But the glut hasn't done to prices what most people would expect.
(Emiliano Zapata, Capital Insiders, via RGV Business Journal, Jan 8, 2026)

## PART 2: PRICES ARE SLIPPING
[VISUAL: price columns (McAllen 2021 / 2023 peak / 2026) with one slow dolly; real street photos of McAllen and Edinburg; stat cards]
**N09:** You might expect all that inventory to send prices tumbling. So far, it hasn't. Prices in the Valley aren't crashing; they're slipping.
*KINETIC: CRASH? · NO. · SLIPPING.*
**N10:** The median listing price in the McAllen metro peaked at about two hundred eighty thousand dollars in October twenty twenty-three. This October it was two hundred fifty-five thousand, roughly nine percent lower, while the median sale price has held near two forty-five since twenty twenty-three.
(Realtor.com via FRED: MEDLISPRI32580; RGV Business Journal, Sep 25, 2026)
**N11:** What has changed is how long homes sit, and how hard sellers have to work. The typical McAllen listing now spends about seventy-eight days on the market, compared with forty-eight in the fall of twenty twenty-one, and about one in six listings had a price cut in October.
(Realtor.com via FRED: days on market Oct 2021 48, Oct 2026 78; 540 price reductions of 3,432 listings)
**N12:** Builders are sweetening deals with rate buy-downs, appliance packages and warranties, and in some cases new homes are now priced below comparable resales. One new three-bedroom on Gumwood Avenue in McAllen, listed at two hundred seventy-five thousand in February, was down to two fifty-nine by August. And then there's the part of the equation nobody in the Valley controls.
(Brian Reed via RGV Business Journal, Feb 25, 2026; RGV Business Journal, Sep 25, 2026)

## PART 3: THE RATE WHIPLASH
[VISUAL: Freddie Mac rate line drawing in; payment columns; real photos of homes and signs]
**N13:** Early this year, buyers caught a break. Rates eased over the winter, January closings in McAllen jumped twenty-one percent from a year earlier, and by late February the thirty-year fixed had dipped to five point nine eight percent.
(RGV Business Journal, Feb 25, 2026; Freddie Mac)
**N14:** Then inflation came back. Energy prices climbed with the conflict involving Iran, rates followed, and by October the thirty-year fixed was back above seven percent.
(NAHB Eye on Housing, Nov 2026; Freddie Mac)
**N15:** Here's what that does to a real budget. On a two-hundred-fifty-five-thousand-dollar home with ten percent down, principal and interest at the February rate comes to about thirteen hundred seventy dollars a month. At today's rate, it's about fifteen hundred seventy, roughly two hundred dollars more every month, before taxes and insurance.
*ON SCREEN: $1,373 → $1,570 / month (RGV News calculation: $229,500 loan, 30-year fixed, principal and interest only)*
**N16:** And sales cooled almost immediately. In September, McAllen metro sales were down eight percent from a year earlier. But rates are only half of the affordability problem.
(RGV Business Journal, Oct 22, 2026: 333 vs 361 sales)

## PART 4: THE AFFORDABILITY GAP
[VISUAL: prices-vs-income columns; RGVBJ document highlight; real photos of Valley neighborhoods]
**N17:** The deeper issue is income. Over the past decade, median home prices in the McAllen metro rose seventy-five percent, while median household income rose sixty-three percent. In Brownsville-Harlingen, prices more than doubled, while incomes rose sixty-seven percent.
(RGV Business Journal, Jul 7, 2026)
**N18:** The median household in Hidalgo County earns about fifty-six thousand dollars a year. Spend thirty percent of that on housing, the usual rule of thumb, and you get about fourteen hundred a month. At today's rates, principal and interest alone on a median-priced home is already more than that.
(U.S. Census Bureau SAIPE 2024 via FRED: $55,829; RGV News calculation)
**N19:** Add property taxes and insurance, and the gap gets wider. One Brownsville broker says deals fall apart when buyers see a monthly note near twenty-eight hundred dollars, and a Valley realtor says some owners are now selling because they can't keep up with the property taxes.
(Craig Grove, GRT Realty, via RGVBJ, Feb 25, 2026; Myssie Cardenas-Barajas via RGVBJ, Jul 7, 2026)
**N20:** Realtors say real demand sits between one hundred fifty and two hundred fifty thousand dollars. But with lots now running fifty to seventy thousand each, builders say a new two-hundred-thousand-dollar house is nearly impossible to pencil out. That's the gap.
(Myssie Cardenas-Barajas via RGVBJ, Jul 7, 2026; Emiliano Zapata via RGVBJ, Jan 8, 2026)

## PART 5: TWO DIFFERENT MARKETS
[VISUAL: real aerial photos of Harlingen neighborhoods; Brownsville photos (Saronic groundbreaking, Sep 30, 2026, White House photo); map of the two metros]
**N21:** And remember, the Valley isn't one housing market. Brownsville-Harlingen has far fewer listings, about fifteen hundred, and roughly six point seven months of inventory, which is close to balanced.
(RGV Business Journal, Sep 25, 2026, August data)
**N22:** Prices there are higher, though. The median Brownsville-Harlingen listing was two hundred ninety-seven thousand dollars in October, down from a peak of three twenty-one in twenty twenty-two.
(Realtor.com via FRED: MEDLISPRI15180)
**N23:** And despite SpaceX and LNG hiring nearby, Brownsville-area home sales fell seventeen percent through August, back to twenty-nineteen levels. Thousands of new jobs haven't turned into home sales, at least not yet.
(RGV Business Journal, Sep 25, 2026)

## PART 6: WHAT TO DO BEFORE 2027
[VISUAL: list cards over real photos (for-sale signs, homes under construction, McAllen skyline); rates chart for the watch list]
**N24:** So what should you do before twenty twenty-seven? If you're buying, this is the most leverage Valley buyers have had in years. Nine months of inventory means you can ask for price cuts, closing costs and rate buy-downs.
**N25:** If you're building, the gap is the opportunity. Townhomes, smaller homes and build-to-rent can reach the one-fifty to two-fifty range that most new single-family homes no longer can.
**N26:** If you're lending, watch carry costs. Banks are tightening construction loans, and some builders are now paying private lenders around thirteen percent, compared with about eight at a bank. A home that sits for nine months at those rates gets expensive.
(Emiliano Zapata via RGV Business Journal, Jan 8, 2026)
**N27:** On our watch list: weekly mortgage rates, McAllen inventory, and whether Brownsville sales pick up as port and SpaceX payrolls grow.
**N28:** The Valley isn't crashing. It's resetting, and in a reset, the deals go to whoever does the math first. This is RGV News.
*END CARD*
