## PART 1: THE BIGGER BRIDGE
[VISUAL: Pharr bridge orbit; USGS aerials of the bridge and Pharr; stat cards]
**N04:** Start on the U.S. side. The Pharr bridge's new second structure adds four lanes, a hundred percent increase in capacity. Bridge director Luis Bazán has said about five thousand trucks cross every day, in both directions.
(RGV Business Journal, Oct 8, 2026 and Dec 30, 2025)
**N05:** Pre-cleared cargo got its own fast lanes in March, and a twenty-acre truck park with about three hundred spaces is expected in early twenty twenty-seven.
(Texas Border Business, 2026)
**N06:** The Texas Comptroller puts the bridge's trade at about fifty billion dollars a year, supporting about ninety-two thousand jobs. At Pharr's first Gateway Forum this fall, business leaders said data centers, manufacturing and produce would drive the next wave.
(RGV Business Journal, Oct 8, 2026)
**N07:** The bigger question they raised was whether the roads around the bridge can keep up. As we covered in Episode 6, the truck corridor behind the Pharr and Donna bridges is still waiting on its funding.
(RGV Business Journal, Oct 8, 2026; Episode 6)
**N08:** That matters for builders, because industrial land next to a bridge is only worth as much as the trucks that can get in and out of it.

## PART 2: THE EMPTY FACTORIES
[VISUAL: Reynosa flyover (new Blender angle over USGS imagery); 3D vacancy columns]
**N09:** Now cross the river. In the first quarter, Reynosa's industrial vacancy reached about ten point four percent, the highest the data firm DATOZ has recorded since twenty sixteen. That's about four point two million square feet sitting empty.
*ON SCREEN: Reynosa 10.41% (Q1 2026) · 7.97% (2024 peak) · Matamoros 3.19%*
(RGV Business Journal, Jun 22, 2026, citing DATOZ)
**N10:** DATOZ says it isn't a wave of speculative building. It's move-outs, corporate adjustments and expansions put on hold while companies wait on the trade rules. Other trackers put the rate lower, around eight percent, so treat it as a range.
(RGV Business Journal, Jun 22, 2026; Newmark; Solili)
**N11:** There have been real losses. Reynosa lost about twenty-five hundred maquila jobs early this year as U.S. demand for e-commerce goods and auto parts slowed, and one of its largest logistics facilities shut down in March, citing tariffs on both sides of the border.
(RGV Business Journal, Mar 4 and Mar 24, 2026)
**N12:** But it isn't one-way. Manufacturers leased more than five hundred fifty thousand square feet in Reynosa and Matamoros over the past year, up fifty-nine percent, led by electronics. And Vertiv, which builds power equipment for AI data centers, announced a hundred-fifty-million-dollar expansion in Reynosa with about a thousand jobs.
(RGV Business Journal, Jun 22 and Jul 5, 2026)
**N13:** And across from Brownsville, Matamoros is much tighter, with industrial vacancy around three percent.
(RGV Business Journal, Jun 22, 2026, citing DATOZ)
**N14:** So the story across the river isn't a collapse. It's a pause, and the companies that are still moving are picky about where they go.

## PART 3: A TRADE DEAL ON A ONE-YEAR CLOCK
[VISUAL: Donna bridge at golden hour; quote card]
**N15:** Behind the hesitation is the trade deal itself. On July first, the U.S. declined to renew the U.S.-Mexico-Canada Agreement for the long term. The deal stays in force, but it now goes through a review every year.
(RGV Business Journal, Jul 1, 2026)
**N16:** Officials said there were no immediate changes to tariffs or customs procedures. But Congressman Henry Cuellar says the decision has frozen multi-million-dollar cross-border investments at South Texas ports of entry.
(RGV Business Journal, Jul 1, 2026; Rio Grande Guardian)
**N17:** Mexico's economy secretary has described it as a ten-year deal with an annual review, which doesn't quite match Washington's reading. For anyone signing a ten-year lease or a construction loan on the border, that gap is the risk.
(Rio Times, Oct 2026, citing Marcelo Ebrard; RGV Business Journal)
**N18:** That uncertainty is showing up on this side of the river too. As we reported in Episode 7, Valley trucking companies have been filing for bankruptcy, and business bankruptcies across the Valley doubled last year.
(RGV Business Journal, Nov 23 and Mar 7, 2026)

## PART 4: WHAT'S STILL MOVING
[VISUAL: Pharr aerials; Port of Brownsville flyover; Mission/Harlingen aerials]
**N19:** Some investment isn't waiting. Pharr is moving to add three hundred eighty-four acres near its international bridge to its tax increment zone, with the vote slated by the end of December.
(RGV Business Journal, Nov 23, 2026)
**N20:** In Mission, metal manufacturer Bettcher is expanding with a three-million-dollar investment and about fifty new jobs. And the Port of Harlingen has launched a twelve-and-a-half-million-dollar infrastructure push to handle more trade.
(RGV Business Journal, Apr 30 and Jun 28, 2026)
**N21:** Even Uber Freight is scouting the Valley, looking to compete for local truck drivers.
(RGV Business Journal, May 26 and Jun 12, 2026)

## PART 5: WHAT IT MEANS FOR BUILDERS, BUYERS AND LENDERS
[VISUAL: Pharr bridge orbit; checklist cards]
**N22:** If you build, the safer industrial bets sit near the new bridge capacity and the truck routes that are actually funded. Speculative space is riskier while tenants across the river have their pick.
**N23:** If you buy or invest, watch who is signing leases, not who is cutting ribbons. Electronics and data-center suppliers are leasing. E-commerce and auto parts have pulled back.
**N24:** If you lend, trade-dependent borrowers like trucking, warehousing and customs brokerage now live on the trade deal's yearly review cycle. Ask how a tenant's business holds up if tariffs change at the next review.
**N25:** And watch the calendar: Pharr's zone expansion vote by the end of December, the new truck park in early twenty twenty-seven, and the trade deal's next annual review.
(RGV Business Journal, Nov 23, 2026; Texas Border Business; RGV Business Journal, Jul 1, 2026)
**N26:** The bridge is ready for more trade. Whether that trade shows up depends on decisions made in Washington and Mexico City, not in Pharr. This is RGV News. This video is for information only and isn't financial advice.
