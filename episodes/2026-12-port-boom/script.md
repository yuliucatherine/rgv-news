## PART 1: WHAT'S BEING BUILT (0:45–2:15)
[VISUAL: 3D ship channel with a depth gauge dropping from 42 to 52 feet; a big ship model slides in]
**N03:** Start with the water. This summer, the Brownsville Ship Channel was deepened from forty-two feet to fifty-two, one of the deepest on the Gulf, deep enough for much larger ships.
**N04:** The project cost more than half a billion dollars, and most of it came from a single tenant: NextDecade, the company building Rio Grande LNG, which paid about four hundred million.
**N04b:** NextDecade leases about nine hundred eighty-four acres from the port on a thirty-year term. That's how big this one project is.
(RGV Business Journal, Jun 26, 2026)
**N05:** Traffic was already booming. Port tenants moved about twenty-eight million tons of cargo in fiscal twenty twenty-five, roughly double what they moved five years earlier.
*ON SCREEN: 28 million tons, FY2025 · +101% in five years*
(RGV Business Journal, Jun 26, 2026)
**N05b:** For comparison, Houston's main channel is forty-five feet deep, and Corpus Christi's is fifty-four. Brownsville can now handle much bigger vessels, including container ships it couldn't take before.
(RGV Business Journal, Jun 26, 2026)
**N06:**
 Then there's the defense industry. Saronic, an autonomous-ship company, is building a three-point-two-billion-dollar shipyard at the port. Governor Abbott says it could eventually employ about ten thousand people.
(KRGV; Office of the Governor)
**N06b:** Port leaders say more than thirty billion dollars in potential projects are in the pipeline, across energy, manufacturing, aerospace, and defense. Potential is the key word. Not all of it will get built.
(Port of Brownsville board vice chairman, State of the Port address, Jun 2026)

## PART 2: THE CONSTRUCTION WAVE (2:15–3:45)
*KINETIC: 7,500 · WORKERS · AT · PEAK*
**N07:** This spring, with global gas supplies squeezed, Rio Grande LNG asked federal regulators to speed things up, and in April they said yes: a peak workforce of seventy-five hundred, and construction around the clock.
(RGV Business Journal, Apr 7, 2026; FERC approval Apr 10, 2026, via Reuters / LNG Prime / Pipeline & Gas Journal)
**N08:** That many workers need places to sleep, eat, and park. Rentals, hotels, RV parks, restaurants, and contractors all feel it.
**N08b:** But not all of them are newcomers. NextDecade says about three-quarters of its onsite workforce has been local, people who already live in the Valley and already have homes.
(NextDecade: about 75% of onsite workforce local, Jul 2023–Jun 2026)
**N09:** But here's the cliff. Once the plant is running, NextDecade says it will support roughly seven hundred permanent jobs.
[VISUAL: 3D columns: 7,500 construction peak vs 700 permanent, the tall column shrinking]
*ON SCREEN: Peak construction about 7,500 → permanent about 700*
(NextDecade fact sheet; RGVBJ, Aug 5, 2026)
**N10:** That's not a knock on the project. Seven hundred permanent, well-paid jobs is a big deal. But demand built for a seventy-five-hundred-person construction crew won't all still be there in twenty twenty-eight.
**N10b:** The wild card is expansion. If NextDecade gets the green light for more trains, construction could keep going well past twenty twenty-seven. That's the scenario to watch.

## PART 3: WHAT LASTS (3:45–5:00)
**N11:** So what lasts? The deeper channel does. Bigger ships mean more cargo, and more cargo means warehouses, trucking, and logistics jobs that don't end when a contractor leaves.
**N12:** Saronic's shipyard could be the biggest long-term employer, if the full build-out happens. Watch its hiring timeline closely, because ten thousand jobs is a projection, not a payroll.
**N13:** And next door, SpaceX reported more than thirty-four hundred workers at Starbase, and Cameron County's judge said the company signaled plans for about four thousand more this year.
(RGVBJ, Aug 5, 2026, citing Cameron County figures)
**N13b:** The county also estimated about twenty-one thousand indirect jobs tied to SpaceX. Estimates like that are worth checking, but they show how much of the local economy now orbits a few big employers.

## PART 4: THE CONSTRAINTS (5:00–5:45)
*KINETIC: POWER · ROADS · WATER*
**N14:** All of this runs into limits. The Valley sits at the end of the line on the Texas power grid. ERCOT found possible reliability problems once peak demand here reaches about thirty-two hundred megawatts.
(RGVBJ, Aug 5, 2026, citing ERCOT study)
**N14b:** Power comes in from the north on long transmission lines, with little generation inside the region and none planned. For a heavy industrial plant or a big new subdivision, that matters.
**N15:** Add a two-lane road to Starbase and a river under strain, as we covered in our last two episodes, and growth here is a race between investment and infrastructure.

## PART 5: WHAT IT MEANS FOR BUILDERS (5:45–6:45)
**N16:** So what does this mean if you build, buy, or lend near the port?
**N17:** Separate temporary demand from permanent demand. Construction crews favor short-term rentals and flexible housing. Permanent workers buy homes and enroll kids in school.
**N18:** Before you commit, ask three questions. Is your demand tied to a construction schedule or a payroll? When does that schedule end? And which permanent employers are actually hiring today?
*LIST: Ask before you build near the port*
**N18b:** The opportunities are real: workforce housing near Highway 48, industrial and logistics space along the deeper channel, and the services every new payroll needs. The key is matching the product to the job that will still be there.
**N18c:** Our watch list: federal decisions on more LNG trains, Saronic's actual hiring, and the grid upgrades ERCOT is planning for the Valley. Each one can move demand by thousands of jobs.
**N19:** The port is building something real. The winners will be the projects sized for what stays after twenty twenty-seven. This is RGV News.
*END CARD*
